\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}
% ============================================================

An expanding-window cross-validated ensemble of three classical time-series forecasters
(\textbf{Theta}, \textbf{Holt-Winters}, \textbf{Hybrid ARIMA}) predicts daily Revenue and
COGS for 2023--2024. Ensemble weights are jointly optimised across three chronological folds
(2020, 2021, 2022) to minimise a combined objective of RMSE (45\%), MAE (35\%), and
$(1 - R^2)$ (20\%).

\medskip
Average cross-validated performance:
\[
  \textbf{MAE} = 522{,}663 \qquad \textbf{RMSE} = 731{,}302 \qquad \boldsymbol{R^2} = 0.7778
\]

Classical time-series models are used instead of tree-based or neural models because the test
horizon (2023--2024) requires extrapolation beyond the training range (2012--2022). Tree
ensembles such as LightGBM cannot predict values outside the training distribution and
systematically under-forecast a growing series. Neural models without explicit trend covariates
exhibit the same limitation and were ruled out after holdout testing.

\section{Pipeline and Feature Engineering}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Input Preprocessing}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Daily sales data is sorted strictly by Date and treated as a contiguous time series.
  \item Missing or zero values inside training windows are linearly interpolated, then
        forward/back-filled. Interpolation is applied independently per fold so no future data
        influences any training step.
  \item All target values are clipped to non-negative before model input.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cyclic Encoding for Explainability}

Raw integer calendar features (e.g., $\text{doy} \in \{1,\ldots,365\}$) create an artificial
discontinuity at the year boundary: the model treats $\text{doy} = 365$ and $\text{doy} = 1$
as maximally distant, even though they are one day apart. Cyclic encoding eliminates this
artefact:
\[
  \text{doy\_sin} = \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot \text{doy}}{365.25}\right), \qquad
  \text{doy\_cos} = \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot \text{doy}}{365.25}\right)
\]
\[
  \text{dow\_sin} = \sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot \text{dow}}{7}\right), \qquad
  \text{dow\_cos} = \cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi \cdot \text{dow}}{7}\right)
\]

One-hot encoding is \textbf{not} used because it would produce 365 or 7 sparse binary columns
and would still not encode cyclic distance correctly. The $(\sin, \cos)$ pair is the minimal
representation that preserves ordinal distance around the cycle.

\subsection{Post-Prediction Postprocessing}

After ensemble blending, predictions pass through a fixed deterministic pipeline:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Multiply by \texttt{SCALE\_REVENUE} / \texttt{SCALE\_COGS} (systematic
        under-prediction correction calibrated on CV).
  \item Apply day-of-week adjustment:
        \[
          y' = y \times \bigl(1 + \delta_{\text{DoW}} \times (f_{\text{dow}} - 1)\bigr),
          \quad \delta_{\text{DoW}} = 0.40
        \]
  \item Add daily bias terms \texttt{REVENUE\_BIAS} and \texttt{COGS\_BIAS}.
  \item Clip to $\geq 0$.
  \item Cap $\text{COGS} \leq 1.05 \times \text{Revenue}$ (competition constraint).
  \item Round to 2 decimal places.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Time-Series Cross-Validation (Expanding Window)}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Method:} Expanding-window time-series cross-validation over three chronological folds.

Unlike $k$-fold cross-validation --- which shuffles rows randomly and allows future
observations to appear in the training set --- expanding-window CV preserves strict temporal
order. The training window grows with each fold; no validation-year data is ever seen during
training.

\textbf{Why Expanding Window and not Rolling Window?}
A rolling window discards older observations, losing the long-run growth trend that is critical
for accurate extrapolation to 2023--2024. The expanding window uses all available history so
the trend estimate improves with each fold.

\section{Leakage Control}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Definition:} data leakage occurs when information from the validation or test period is
used --- directly or indirectly --- during model training or hyperparameter selection. This
inflates in-sample metrics and produces models that fail on unseen data.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{L{4.5cm} L{9cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Control point} & \textbf{Implementation} \\
\midrule
No future Revenue/COGS in training &
  Each fold filters strictly to \texttt{Date < fold\_year}; test Revenue/COGS are never
  loaded at any stage. \\
\addlinespace
No global statistics from test data &
  Scale factors, means, and standard deviations are computed only from the training portion
  of each fold. \\
\addlinespace
DoW profiles recomputed per fold &
  DoW multipliers use only \texttt{train[Date.year < fold\_year]}; the 2022 profile does
  not see 2022 actuals. \\
\addlinespace
Interpolation within fold only &
  Missing values are filled using each fold's training window alone; no forward-fill from
  future observations. \\
\addlinespace
Ensemble weights from out-of-fold validation &
  Softmax weights are optimised against fold validation errors, not the 2023--2024 test
  horizon. \\
\addlinespace
Sample submission used only for dates &
  The test CSV supplies future dates only; its Revenue/COGS columns are ignored. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Leakage control measures applied at every pipeline stage.}
\end{table}

\section{Feature Importance and Explainability}
% ============================================================

\textbf{Why not SHAP?}
The production model is an ensemble of classical statistical forecasters (Theta, ARIMA). SHAP
via TreeSHAP requires a gradient-boosted tree structure; DeepSHAP requires a neural network.
Applying kernel SHAP to a time-series forecaster would require treating each forecast step as
an independent sample, which discards the temporal autocorrelation structure the models are
specifically designed to exploit. Absolute Pearson correlation between each calendar feature
and Revenue/COGS is used instead as a deterministic, interpretable importance proxy.

\textbf{Key insights:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{doy\_cos / doy\_sin} dominate because fashion e-commerce demand follows a
        strong annual cycle. Cyclic encoding captures this without the artificial discontinuity
        of raw integer day-of-year.
  \item \textbf{is\_month\_end} ($\approx$13\%) indicates consistent end-of-month purchasing
        spikes, consistent with payroll-cycle consumer behavior and monthly promotional
        campaigns.
  \item \textbf{days\_since} + \textbf{year} together ($\approx$21\%) confirm long-run
        business growth is the largest driver of absolute Revenue level --- the primary reason
        tree ensembles fail on this task.
  \item \textbf{dom} ($\approx$10\%) reinforces the monthly periodicity signal that
        complements \texttt{is\_month\_end}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Optimised Ensemble Weights}
% ============================================================

The ensemble objective is:
\[
  \mathcal{L} = 0.45 \cdot \frac{\text{RMSE}}{735{,}000}
              + 0.35 \cdot \frac{\text{MAE}}{532{,}000}
              + 0.20 \cdot (1 - R^2)

Weights are found via Nelder-Mead minimisation with 12 random restarts over

\section{Mathematical Formulas}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Competition Metrics}

\[
  \text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |F_i - A_i|
\]
\[
  \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (F_i - A_i)^2}
\]
\[
  R^2 = 1 - \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_i - F_i)^2}
             {\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(A_i - \bar{A})^2}
\]

where $F_i$ is the forecast, $A_i$ the actual, and $\bar{A}$ the mean of actuals.
$n = 2 \times 365 = 730$ (Revenue and COGS concatenated).

\subsection{Theta Method}

The Theta method \cite{theta2000} decomposes $y_t$ into two modified theta lines:
\[
  \tilde{y}_0(t) = 2\bar{y} - y_t \quad \text{(suppresses seasonality, retains linear trend)}
\]
\[
  \tilde{y}_2(t) = y_t \quad \text{(retains the full series)}
\]
The $h$-step forecast is:
\[
  F(h) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{SES}(\tilde{y}_0,\, h) + \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b(T + h))
\]
where SES denotes simple exponential smoothing on $\tilde{y}_0$, and $a, b$ are the OLS
intercept and slope of $\tilde{y}_0$. Applied on $\log(1 + y)$; output is
$e^{F(h)} - 1$ multiplied by the learned day-of-week factor.

\subsection{Holt-Winters (Multiplicative Seasonality)}

\begin{align*}
  L_t &= \alpha \frac{y_t}{S_{t-m}} + (1-\alpha)(L_{t-1} + B_{t-1}) \\
  B_t &= \beta (L_t - L_{t-1}) + (1-\beta) B_{t-1} \\
  S_t &= \gamma \frac{y_t}{L_t} + (1-\gamma) S_{t-m} \\
  F(h) &= (L_T + h B_T)\, S_{T+h - m\lceil h/m \rceil}
\end{align*}

with $m = 7$ (weekly) and $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ optimised by SSE minimisation.

\subsection{Hybrid ARIMA}

\textbf{Step 1 --- Seasonal baseline:}
\[
  \hat{y}(t) = b \cdot g^{\Delta_{\text{yr}}} \cdot s(\text{month}, \text{day})
\]
where $b$ is the mean daily level of the last full training year, $g$ is the geometric mean
of the last 3 annual year-over-year growth rates, and $s$ is the seasonal profile
$\bar{y}_{\text{(month,day)}} / \bar{y}_{\text{annual}}$.

\textbf{Step 2 --- Residual ARIMA$(p, 0, q)$:}
\[
  e_t = y_t - \hat{y}(t), \qquad
  e_t = c + \sum_{i=1}^{p} \varphi_i e_{t-i} + \sum_{j=1}^{q} \theta_j \varepsilon_{t-j} + \varepsilon_t
\]
Fit on residuals from the last 3 training years only.

\textbf{Step 3 --- Combine:}
\[
  F(h) = \max\!\bigl(0,\; \hat{y}(h) + \text{ARIMA\_forecast}(h)\bigr)
\]

\subsection{Ensemble Objective}

Let $\mathbf{w}_{\text{rev}} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{1:3})$ and
$\mathbf{w}_{\text{cog}} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{4:6})$ for unconstrained
$\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^6$. For each fold year $k$:
\[
  \hat{\mathbf{v}}_k = \begin{pmatrix}
    \text{postprocess}\!\left(\textstyle\sum_i w_{\text{rev},i}\, \hat{r}_{k,i}\right) \\
    \text{postprocess}\!\left(\textstyle\sum_j w_{\text{cog},j}\, \hat{c}_{k,j}\right)
  \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
  \mathbf{a}_k = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{r}_k \\ \mathbf{c}_k \end{pmatrix}
\]
\[
  \mathcal{L}_k = 0.45 \cdot \frac{\text{RMSE}(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_k, \mathbf{a}_k)}{735{,}000}
                + 0.35 \cdot \frac{\text{MAE}(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_k, \mathbf{a}_k)}{532{,}000}
                + 0.20 \cdot (1 - R^2(\hat{\mathbf{v}}_k, \mathbf{a}_k))
\]
\[
  \min_{\mathbf{z}}\; \frac{1}{3}\sum_{k \in \{2020,2021,2022\}} \mathcal{L}_k
  \quad \text{via Nelder-Mead, 12 random restarts}
\]
